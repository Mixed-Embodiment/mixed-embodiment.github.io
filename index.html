Mix and Match: Amplifying Human Demonstrations with Mixed-Embodiment Data

TL;DR: Mixed human–robot demonstrations bridge scalable human experience and robot-specific action supervision, improving bimanual manipulation learning under fixed demonstration budgets.

Abstract

Imitation learning for robot manipulation faces a data tradeoff. Human demonstrations are cheap and scalable but carry no labels in the robot’s action space, while teleoperated demonstrations provide exact action supervision at a far higher collection cost. Existing approaches typically treat these as separately collected domains, leaving the relationship between human and robot behavior to be inferred during learning. We introduce Mixed-Embodiment Demonstrations as bridge data between human and robot embodiments, designed to make deployment-stage adaptation from human demonstrations to robot control more sample-efficient. By observing human and robot behavior together within the same bimanual task execution, mixed demonstrations provide direct cross-embodiment supervision that is absent when the two domains are collected independently. We evaluate our Mix and Match pipeline across three real-world bimanual manipulation tasks. Mix and Match consistently outperforms comparable combinations of independently collected human and robot demonstrations, improving task success by 35 percentage points on average. We further show that mixed data supports strong performance with substantially less human-only data and improves adaptation from human-pretrained policies. These results suggest that a relatively small amount of mixed-embodiment data may help bridge scalable human experience and robot-specific control more efficiently.

Framework

Mix and Match Overview

System Architecture

Human wrist positions are recovered using WiLoR and depth; robot end-effector positions are obtained from forward kinematics. Both are expressed in a common workspace frame. An embodiment mask controls the availability of joint-state inputs and robot action supervision, while relative pose supervision is shared across all demonstration types.

Results

Evaluation Tasks

Real-Robot Policy Evaluation

Matched-Budget Co-Training

Replacing 10 HH and 10 RR demonstrations with 20 mixed demonstrations preserves the number of human trajectories, robot trajectories, and total episodes. Mixed demonstrations improve success on all three tasks, with gains of 15–55 percentage points and an average gain of 35 percentage points.

Replacing Human-Only Demonstrations

For Fold, 200 HH and 30 RR demonstrations achieve 5% success; 150 HH, 20 mixed, and 30 RR demonstrations achieve 60%. For Insertion, with 40 RR demonstrations fixed, replacing part of the HH data with mixed data increases success from 25% to 65%.

Fine-Tuning from Human Demonstrations

Starting from the same 200-HH Fold checkpoint, fine-tuning with 30 RR demonstrations reaches 25% success, while 15 RR and 14 mixed demonstrations reach 50%.

Representation Analysis

PCA projections of encoder representations show that held-out human and robot observations become less separated as the amount of mixed data increases. This is a qualitative observation rather than a conclusive measure of alignment.

Preliminary Mixed-Embodiment Execution

In five informal trials per task with one experienced operator, the policy completed each task two to three times. These preliminary results are qualitative and do not constitute a controlled human-subject study.
